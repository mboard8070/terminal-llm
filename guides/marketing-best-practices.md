# Marketing Best Practices

## Strategy Fundamentals

- **Know your audience before you create anything.** Demographics (age, income, location) are a start. Psychographics (values, pain points, aspirations) are what actually drive decisions. Build a real customer profile, not a vague persona.
- **One message per campaign.** If you're saying three things, you're saying nothing. Distill to one clear value proposition. "Fastest delivery in the city" not "Fast delivery, great prices, and friendly service."
- **Position against alternatives, not just competitors.** Your competition isn't just direct rivals — it's the status quo, doing nothing, and DIY solutions. Address why change is worth it.
- **Differentiate on something defensible.** Price is the weakest differentiator — someone can always go lower. Differentiate on expertise, speed, experience, niche focus, or trust.
- **Marketing without a funnel is just noise.** Every piece of content should map to a stage: Awareness (they don't know you), Consideration (they're comparing), Decision (they're ready to buy), Retention (they already bought).

## Content Marketing

- **Teach, don't pitch.** Content that solves a real problem earns trust. Content that only talks about your product earns an unsubscribe.
- **Headlines do 80% of the work.** If the headline doesn't hook, the content doesn't matter. Test multiple headlines. Use numbers, specificity, and curiosity gaps.
- **Write for skimmers first, readers second.** Bold key phrases, use subheadings every 2-3 paragraphs, front-load the value. Most people scan before deciding to read.
- **Evergreen over trending.** A "How to Choose the Right X" guide generates traffic for years. A hot take on today's news is worthless next week. Invest accordingly.
- **Repurpose everything.** One blog post becomes a LinkedIn carousel, a Twitter thread, a newsletter section, a YouTube script, and 5 social media quotes. Create once, distribute many.
- **Consistency beats virality.** Publishing weekly for a year beats one viral post. Algorithms and audiences reward reliability.

## Social Media

### Platform Strategy
- **LinkedIn:** Professional insights, behind-the-scenes, case studies, industry opinions. Long-form text posts outperform links. Best engagement: Tuesday-Thursday mornings.
- **X (Twitter):** Hot takes, threads, quick tips, engagement with others. Short and punchy. Reply to bigger accounts in your niche to get visibility.
- **Instagram:** Visual storytelling, Reels, carousel education posts, Stories for daily engagement. Aesthetic consistency matters. Use alt text.
- **TikTok:** Authentic, fast-paced, trend-aware. First 1-2 seconds determine if someone keeps watching. Hook immediately. Embrace imperfection — overproduced content underperforms.
- **YouTube:** Long-form value. Invest in thumbnails (they're more important than the video title). First 30 seconds must prove the video is worth watching.

### Content Principles
- **Hook in the first line.** On every platform, the first sentence determines whether anyone reads the rest. Lead with a surprising stat, a bold claim, or a direct question.
- **End with a call to action.** "Follow for more," "Save this for later," "Drop your answer below," "Link in bio." Tell people what to do next.
- **Engage, don't just broadcast.** Reply to every comment for the first hour. Comment on others' posts. Social media rewards conversations, not megaphones.
- **Use native content, not links.** Platforms suppress posts that send people away. Upload videos directly, write text posts instead of linking to blogs, use carousels instead of linking to slides.
- **Post frequency guidelines:** LinkedIn 3-5x/week, X 1-3x/day, Instagram 4-7x/week (mix feed + Stories + Reels), TikTok 1-3x/day, YouTube 1-2x/week.

### Hashtags & Discovery
- **3-5 hashtags on LinkedIn.** Mix broad (#Marketing) with niche (#B2BSaaS). Don't overdo it.
- **Instagram:** 5-15 hashtags. Mix sizes — some with millions of posts (reach), some with thousands (niche visibility). Put them in the caption, not comments.
- **TikTok:** 3-5 trending + niche hashtags. Check the Discover page for current trends.
- **X:** 1-2 max, or zero. Hashtag-heavy tweets look spammy.

## Email Marketing

- **Subject lines are everything.** 40-60 characters. Create urgency, curiosity, or clear value. Personalize with the recipient's name when appropriate. A/B test every send.
- **One email, one goal.** Don't cram a product update, a blog link, a webinar invite, and a survey into one email. Each email should drive one action.
- **Send from a person, not a brand.** "Matt from SleekTiki" gets opened more than "SleekTiki Team." People open emails from people.
- **Segment your list.** New subscribers get a welcome sequence. Active customers get upsells. Inactive users get re-engagement. One-size-fits-all emails underperform segmented ones by 50%+.
- **Welcome sequence is non-negotiable.** 3-5 emails over 2 weeks after signup: introduce yourself, deliver value, share your best content, then soft-sell. First impressions set the tone.
- **Respect the unsubscribe.** Make it one click. Don't guilt-trip. A clean list of engaged subscribers beats a bloated list of people who hate you.
- **Send frequency:** Weekly is the sweet spot for most businesses. Daily works for media/deals. Monthly gets forgotten.

## SEO Basics

- **Write for humans, optimize for search.** If your content is genuinely useful and answers a real question, Google will reward it. Keyword-stuffed garbage ranks temporarily and converts never.
- **Target long-tail keywords.** "Best project management tool for remote teams under 10 people" converts better than "project management tool." Less competition, higher intent.
- **Title tags and meta descriptions matter.** Title: under 60 characters, include primary keyword. Meta description: under 155 characters, include a reason to click.
- **Internal linking builds authority.** Every new page should link to 2-3 related existing pages. Every existing relevant page should link back. This spreads authority and helps users navigate.
- **Page speed is a ranking factor.** Compress images, minimize JavaScript, use a CDN. A 1-second delay in load time reduces conversions by 7%.
- **Update old content.** Refresh top-performing posts with new data, updated screenshots, and current dates. Google rewards freshness. Add "[Updated 2026]" to titles.

## Copywriting

- **Features tell, benefits sell.** "256GB storage" is a feature. "Never delete a photo again" is a benefit. Always translate features into what they mean for the customer.
- **Use the customer's language.** Read reviews, support tickets, Reddit threads, and forum posts. Mirror the exact words your audience uses to describe their problems.
- **Social proof near the CTA.** Testimonials, case studies, user counts, ratings — place them right before the buy/signup button. Proof reduces friction at the decision point.
- **Urgency must be real.** Fake countdown timers and "Only 2 left!" when you have 2,000 units erodes trust permanently. Real urgency (limited enrollment, seasonal pricing, actual deadlines) works.
- **Power words that drive action:** Free, new, proven, instant, exclusive, guaranteed, limited, discover, secret, simple. Use sparingly — they lose power when overused.
- **PAS framework:** Problem (name the pain), Agitate (make them feel it), Solution (your offer). Works for emails, landing pages, ads, and social posts.
- **AIDA framework:** Attention (hook), Interest (relevance), Desire (benefits), Action (CTA). The classic for longer-form copy.

## Paid Advertising

- **Start small, scale what works.** $10-20/day per ad set to test. Kill losers fast (48-72 hours with no conversions). Double down on winners.
- **Creative matters more than targeting.** Platforms have gotten excellent at finding your audience. Your job is to make creative that stops the scroll.
- **Test one variable at a time.** Headline, image, audience, CTA — change one thing per test. Changing everything tells you nothing about what worked.
- **Retargeting is the highest ROI.** People who visited your site, watched your video, or engaged with your content are 3-5x more likely to convert. Always run retargeting campaigns.
- **Landing page must match the ad.** If the ad says "50% off running shoes," the landing page should show running shoes at 50% off. Not your homepage. Not your full catalog. Matching = conversions.
- **Track everything.** UTM parameters on every link. Conversion pixels on every thank-you page. If you can't measure it, you can't improve it.

## Analytics & Measurement

- **Define success before you start.** "Get more followers" is not a goal. "Reach 1,000 newsletter subscribers by June" is. Specific, measurable, time-bound.
- **Vanity metrics vs. real metrics:**
  - **Vanity:** Followers, impressions, page views, likes
  - **Real:** Conversion rate, cost per acquisition, revenue per customer, email open/click rate, return on ad spend
- **Attribution is messy — accept it.** A customer might see your tweet, read your blog, get your email, and convert from a Google search. Multi-touch attribution matters, but don't obsess over perfect tracking.
- **Review weekly, optimize monthly, strategize quarterly.** Check dashboards weekly. Make tactical changes monthly. Revisit strategy every quarter.
- **Benchmarks by channel:**
  - Email open rate: 20-25% is good, 30%+ is great
  - Email click rate: 2-5% is good
  - Social engagement rate: 1-3% is average, 5%+ is strong
  - Paid ads CTR: 1-2% for display, 3-5% for search, 1-3% for social
  - Landing page conversion: 2-5% is average, 10%+ is excellent

## Brand Voice

- **Document your voice in 3-5 adjectives.** "Confident, clear, slightly irreverent" or "Warm, technical, no-nonsense." Every piece of content should pass the voice check.
- **Create a "we say / we don't say" list.** "We say 'team members,' not 'resources.' We say 'challenge,' not 'problem.' We don't use corporate jargon."
- **Voice stays consistent, tone adapts.** Voice is your personality (always the same). Tone changes with context — a product launch is excited, a service outage is empathetic, a tutorial is patient.
- **Read it aloud.** Does it sound like your brand talking? Or does it sound like a generic corporate blog? If you can swap your company name with a competitor's and it still works, your voice isn't distinctive enough.

## Marketing Checklist

Before launching a campaign, verify:

1. Is there a clear, single objective (not 3 goals crammed together)?
2. Do you know exactly who you're targeting and why they'd care?
3. Is the value proposition specific and differentiated?
4. Does every piece of content have a clear CTA?
5. Is tracking in place (UTMs, conversion pixels, analytics)?
6. Are you measuring real metrics, not vanity metrics?
7. Is there a follow-up plan (retargeting, email sequence, nurture)?
8. Does the content match your brand voice?
